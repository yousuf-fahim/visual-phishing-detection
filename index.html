Screenshot Phishing Score

A small neural network (MobileNetV3) trained on screenshots from the Phish360 research dataset estimates how much a web page looks like the phishing pages it was trained on. It never sees the page's address, so it cannot know whether a site is genuine.

What the score means

The score is the model's probability that the screenshot comes from the "phishing" class of its training data. It is a statement about appearance only. A high score means the page is visually similar to phishing pages in the dataset, which in practice often just means "a sparse page with a login form". A low score means it looks more like the dataset's legitimate pages, which were mostly busy homepages.

Known limits (from our report)

  • It confuses "login page" with "phishing". Real sign-in pages of banks and email providers often score high. On test pages where simple page statistics point the wrong way, ROC-AUC falls to .
  • Faithful clones of busy homepages slip through. They look like the legitimate training pages.

  • Other languages. A version trained on English pages scored on English and on non-English pages.
  • Not tested against attackers. Someone who knows the model could design a page to fool it.
